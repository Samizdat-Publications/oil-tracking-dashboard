# Design brief — V5, "The bill Trump handed you"

**Date:** 2026-09-05 (rev 2 — full aesthetic redesign authorised; the first revision of
this file kept the V4 poster tokens and is superseded)
**Repo:** `Samizdat-Publications/oil-tracking-dashboard`. Read `README.md`, then this,
then `docs/THESIS.md` before writing a word of copy. Live: https://trumps-economy-ledger.pages.dev
**Screenshots of what exists:** `docs/screenshots/01-…11-*.png`, `m-*.png` (mobile).

This brief makes the concept decisions. Design executes them, with latitude wherever the
brief says "Design decides". Every number below is live in
`frontend/public/data-snapshot.json` and re-derives on each build; treat all figures as
bound placeholders that must survive changing length, never as fixed text.

---

## 0. The decision

**Concept: a tally.** The page is one long receipt being written in front of the reader.
Each chapter is one full-screen number — what a single decision cost — and as the reader
scrolls, a receipt rail adds the line and the total ticks up. At the bottom the receipt is
complete: **+$83 a month, $1,440 so far**, itemised, with his name on it as the person who
ordered it and yours as the person who paid.

Why this and not another poster: the V4 page is eleven loud posters stacked; by the fourth
the skimmer is numb and the deep reader has nowhere quieter to go. A tally solves both. The
skimmer reads the rail. The deep reader opens the work under each chapter. And the
metaphor is the argument — this is a bill, someone ordered it, someone else is paying.

Everything about the V4 look that was a stylistic tic rather than a message carrier goes:
the halftone dot grounds, the hard-offset drop shadows, the crimson-on-cream text-shadow
poster device, the alternating cream/blue/sea section grounds. What stays is discipline:
zero radius, no decoration that is not data, every figure sourced.

## 1. Voice — named, active, inside the data

The current copy hides the subject. "The bill for two choices." "A war ordered in
February." Ordered by whom?

**Rules**
1. **Name him** in every headline or kicker: Trump, or he. Never "the administration",
   "policy", "decisions taken after January 2025".
2. **Active verbs.** He ordered, he imposed, he lost in court and re-imposed the same day,
   he fired, he said.
3. **Second person for the cost.** Your receipt. Your diesel. What you pay a month.
4. **Every adjective is backed by a number on the same screen.** "Record" only where the
   series is literally at a record (diesel; Labor Day gasoline). Otherwise no adjectives.
5. **Claims stay inside the data.** The page may say his war doubled crude in five weeks,
   put diesel at a record, is 14 points of energy inflation and shut a strait that is
   still shut seven months on; his tariffs are a 0.7-point creep in core inflation and a
   court found them illegal; job creation is 42,000 a month against 321,000 with no
   pandemic to blame. It may **not** say he crashed the economy (GDP +1.5%, S&P +29%),
   caused a recession (none) or collapsed the dollar (up since the war began). Those rows
   are on the page as *Against us*, and they are why the rest is believed.
6. **The honest rows keep full size.** Eggs −56%. August +162,000 jobs. Mortgages lower
   than the handover. Gold down 20% from its record. The Fed's counter-argument on gold.
   Never collapsed, never greyed, never smaller.

**Title:** **The bill Trump handed you.**
**Lede:** *He ordered a war in February. He imposed tariffs, lost them in court, and
imposed them again the same day. Seven months later the strait he shut is still shut,
diesel is at a record, and this is your receipt.*

Alternates to typeset for comparison: **He did this.** · **Trump's war. Trump's tariffs.
Your bill.**

## 2. Visual system

### Colour has meaning, and the meaning is the argument

| Colour | Means | Used for | Never used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crimson** | *He decided this.* | The dated acts only: 28 Feb strike, the tariff proclamations, the 8 Jul resumption, the BLS firing, "30 ships a night". Event markers, kickers naming him. | Anything the data did on its own. No red bars, no red numbers. |
| **Amber** | *Measured.* Oil, prices, the money. | Every chart line, every counted figure, the receipt total. | Decoration. |
| **Paper** (cream) | *The receipt.* | The rail, the end card, the till receipt in chapter 3. | Section grounds. |
| **Ink** (near-black) | The ground. | Everything sits on it. | — |
| **Slate blue** | *The control group / against us.* Europe, the counter-arguments, the rows that cut the other way. | Crossing chart's euro line, *Against us* cards, the honest panels' rule. | — |
| **Grey hatch** | *A claim, not data.* | The President's ship count; anything asserted and not measured. | — |

A reader who learns "red means he did it" in chapter one carries that through the page.
Design must not spend red on anything else.

### Type

- **Numbers:** a condensed grotesk at poster scale (200–320px on desktop, 88–120px on
  mobile). The number is the poster. Tabular figures, tight tracking. Design picks the
  face; it must have a true condensed weight and self-host (no Google Fonts at runtime).
- **Argument:** a serious serif for leads and receipt lines, 20–26px. Credibility lives
  here; the reader we most need is the one who distrusts shouting.
- **Sources, dates, tiers:** a mono at 11–13px, uppercase, wide tracking. Every figure has
  one of these beneath it.
- No Chivo, no Instrument Serif (that is V2 and V4; start clean).

### Ground and rhythm

- One continuous ink ground, no alternating section colours. Chapter breaks are marked by
  the number, a thin amber rule and the rail ticking — not by a colour change.
- Each chapter is designed to **fill one viewport at 1400×900 and one at 390×844** with
  its number, its one sentence and its chart. Everything else is below the fold of that
  chapter under *Show the work*.
- Zero border radius. No drop shadows of any kind. Rules are 1px or 3px, never both in
  one component.

### Motion (scroll-driven, once, then still)

- The chapter number counts up as it enters (600–900ms, ease-out); it never re-plays.
- Chart lines draw left to right as the chapter enters.
- The receipt rail adds its line item and the total re-tallies with a visible increment
  (mono, amber) when the chapter's number has finished counting.
- The strait simulation keeps its own engine (`frontend/src/v4/hormuz/engine.js`,
  already running on measured daily closes and IMF PortWatch counts — do not redraw the
  map or the vessel logic; restyle only).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: everything at its end state, complete, no half-built page.

## 3. Two readers, one page

### Skim layer (the rail + the chapter numbers)

- **The receipt rail.** Desktop: a fixed 300–340px column of cream "paper" on the right
  with mono type, a torn top edge if Design wants one gesture of realism and nothing more.
  Each chapter appends a line as it is reached: `WAR · CRUDE ×2 IN 5 WKS`,
  `DIESEL · RECORD $5.60`, `JOBS · 42,474/MO vs 320,938` … and at the bottom a live
  **TOTAL +$83.18 / MONTH · $1,440 SINCE 20 JAN 2025**. Mobile: a collapsed bottom
  sheet showing only the running total, expandable to the itemised list. Design decides
  how it avoids the simulation controls.
- **Chapter numbers** are the skim path: eight screens, eight numbers, one sentence each.
  Reading only the numbers and sentences takes under a minute and gives the whole
  argument.
- **The end card** (chapter 10) is the completed receipt at poster size, laid out to be
  screenshotted on a phone and to be the OG image at 1200×630 from one component.
- **A chapter index** in the rail (or a thin left gutter on desktop): the eight numbers as
  jump targets, not the titles.

### Deep layer (*Show the work*)

- Under every chapter, below its fold, a disclosure labelled **Show the work** — a full-
  width 1px amber rule with the label, not a chevron — opens: the full chart at height,
  method, falsifier ("what would prove this wrong"), the series IDs and tiers, caveats,
  links. All of this exists today as footnotes and callouts; it moves down and in.
- **Never inside a disclosure:** the honest panels, the *Against us* chapter, the missing-
  October note, the two corrections. They are argument, not appendix.

## 4. Chapter order

The arc: *what it costs you → the act → the mechanism → your receipt → his second act →
jobs → your money → the world's verdict → the proof → against us → the bill → check it.*
The control-group chart moves late: it is the proof for the reader who has stayed, and
in position 03 it was a screen of method before anything touched the reader's life.

### 02 · The strait — restyle, don't rebuild

The engine, map, vessel model and the two canvases stay. Restyle the rail of readouts,
the event card, the legend and the scrubber into the new system. Add one readout:
**tankers a day** (`hormuz_transits.observations[].tanker`, baseline 46.6). Make the

honesty label: illustrative geometry, not AIS data, no verified queue count, last
published close.

### 07 · The world routed around America — a flow, not tiles

Six dashboard tiles become one horizontal chain with a number at each node and the
WTO's sentence as the pull quote: *"Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz collapsing from
138 commercial vessels per day to almost zero."* Nodes and keys: strait {mean7}/day
(`hormuz_transits`) → world oil supply −4.3 mb/d, demand −1.6 (`context.trade.iea_august_omr`)
→ diesel $5.60 record (`macro.series.diesel_weekly`) → Shanghai–Jebel Ali $8,254, +6% w/w
(`context.trade.drewry_wci`) → airfares +25.5%, airline profit $41bn → $23bn
(`macro.yoy.cpi_airfares`, `context.trade.iata`) → your receipt. Secondary: US trade
deficit $88.6bn in July, customs duties $364bn → $326bn after the court
(`context.trade.us_trade_july`, `macro.series.customs_duties`). The WTO columns
(4.6 / 1.9 / 1.4 / 2.6) can live in *Show the work* or as the chapter's small chart.

### 08 · The gold left New York — the ounce chart is the hero

The whole chapter is one sentence: *the Fed books foreign gold at $42.22 an ounce, a
price fixed in 1973, so its own table counts ounces — and the ounces have left every
month since August 2025.* Design that sentence as the axis label of the hero chart, not
as a caption.

- **Hero:** ten monthly bars hanging down from a rule — Sep 2025 → Jun 2026: −35, −25,
  −29, −10, −15, 0, −5, −15, −10, −15 tonnes; total **−159 t**; 5,919 → 5,760 t
  (`context.gold.fed_earmarked_gold.points`, tonnes = usd_m / 42.22 × 31.1035 / 1000).
  No gold-bar illustrations; a ledger, not a coin advert.
- **Second, smaller:** Treasuries in Fed custody for foreign officials, monthly change,
  $bn: +69, −7, −57, +7, −59, −50 ($2.78tn → $2.62tn). Mixed signs shown as they are.
- **Four cards:** Netherlands 86 t out of New York and Ottawa, Mar–Aug 2026, "in view of
  increasing geopolitical unrest" (Tier 1, DNB press release) · France 129 t, Jul 2025–
  Jan 2026 · India 55% → 22% of reserves held abroad · **Germany 1,236 t still in New
  York, no plan** — design Germany as *not yet*, not as a fifth departure.
- **The honest panel, full size, pattern "We checked the counter-argument":** the Fed's
  sentence (FEDS Note, 3 Sep 2026: the gold-over-Treasuries headline is a valuation
  effect; foreign officials still bought ~$200bn of Treasuries net since 2022) and the
  ECB's ("mainly valuation"), gold −20% from its 28 Jan record, dollar index up since
  the war — in slate blue, same type size as ours — then our reply: none of that explains
  the ounce count above. Design decides the typographic device; a two-column layout that
  collapses badly on mobile is not it.

## 6. Constraints that do not move

- **No fabricated numbers.** Every figure is bound to a snapshot key or to an entry in
  `backend/data/context_figures.json` with source, URL and tier. Artboard placeholders
  are marked `PLACEHOLDER`.
- **War and tariff effects are never summed.** (Dallas Fed: they roughly cancel.)
- **No queue count.** The vessel layer is illustrative and labelled.
- **The insider-trading probe never shares a frame with the paper-physical spread.**
- **Missing data renders as a gap.** October 2025 CPI does not exist.
- **Tier labels stay visible** where they exist (TIER 1 / TIER 2).
- **Against us and the honest panels are never collapsed, greyed or shrunk.**
- Container-query sizing, no media queries; 390px with zero horizontal overflow; every
  control ≥ 44px; fonts self-hosted.

## 8. Questions for Design

- Where does the running total live on a phone so it never covers the simulation controls?
- Fifteen receipt lines on a 390px screen with fallers visible by default: how?
- Can the crossing chart (chapter 09) become smaller and still carry "below, then above,
  then closing"?
- The "we checked the counter-argument" device in chapter 08: a typographic form that
  gives their sentence and ours equal weight on one column?
- Should the rail's line items be the chapter numbers verbatim, or shorter?

## 9. Acceptance

- A first-time reader on a phone, scrolling for sixty seconds and opening nothing, can
  name the monthly cost, the crude peak, the ship count and the gold figure.
- A hostile reader, opening nothing, can find all six *Against us* rows, both honest
  panels, the missing-October note and both corrections.
- Every red mark on the page is a dated act by Trump.
- Every number on every artboard is bound or marked `PLACEHOLDER`.
- No headline without an actor.
