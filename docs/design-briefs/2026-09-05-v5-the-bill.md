## 1. What the page is, said plainly

A page that shows what Donald Trump's two decisions — the war on Iran he ordered on
28 February 2026 and the tariffs he imposed, lost in court, and re-imposed — have cost
American households, measured entirely in published government statistics.

It is not neutral about who decided. It is scrupulous about what the numbers say. The
second thing is what makes the first thing land: the page's whole credibility with a
sceptical reader rests on the rows that cut against it being shown at full size, and on
every figure carrying its source. **Sharpen the voice; do not soften the honesty.**

---

## 2. Voice — from passive to named

The current copy is passive to the point of hiding the subject. "The bill for two
choices." "A war ordered in February." "Tariffs imposed, struck down, and re-imposed."
Ordered by whom? Imposed by whom? Every headline should have an actor and a verb.

### Rules

1. **Name him.** "Trump" or "he" in every section headline or kicker. Not "the
   administration", not "policy", not "decisions taken after January 2025".
2. **Active verbs.** He ordered, he imposed, he re-imposed, he fired (the BLS
   commissioner), he said (the strait is open). The data then shows what followed.
3. **Second person for the cost.** The reader pays. "Your diesel." "Your receipt."
   "What you pay a month."
4. **Every adjective is backed by a number on the same card.** No "devastating",
   "historic", "catastrophic" unless the number beside it is literally a record
   (diesel and gasoline on Labor Day are; use "record" there and nowhere else).
5. **Claims stay inside the data.** The page may say his war doubled the price of crude
   in five weeks, put diesel at a record, is 14 points of energy inflation, and shut a
   strait that is still shut seven months later. It may say his tariffs are a persistent
   0.7-point creep in core inflation and that a court found them illegal. It may say job
   creation is 42,000 a month against 321,000 with no pandemic to blame. It may **not**
   say he crashed the economy (GDP +1.5%, S&P 500 +29%), caused a recession (none), or
   collapsed the dollar (the index is up since the war began). Those rows are on the page
   as "the other side", and they are why the rest is believed.
6. **The honest rows keep their size.** Eggs down 56%. August payrolls +162,000.
   Mortgages lower than the handover. Gold down 20% from its record. The Fed's own
   counter-argument on gold. These are not footnotes. A reader who finds them buried
   stops believing the page.

### Title and lede — candidates

Current: **The bill for two choices** / *A war ordered in February. Tariffs imposed,
struck down, and re-imposed…*

Preferred: **The bill Trump handed you**
*He ordered a war in February. He imposed tariffs, lost them in court, and imposed them
again the same day. Seven months later the strait he shut is still shut, diesel is at a
record, and this is your receipt.*

Alternates for Design to test at poster size: **He did this.** (H1 alone, receipt below) ·
**Trump's war. Trump's tariffs. Your bill.** · **What Trump's war costs you** (drops the
tariffs from the title; keeps them in section 05).

Keep the crimson-with-cream-hard-shadow poster device on the operative word.

## 3. Two readers, one page

Right now every section exposes everything: chart, callout, caveats, footnote. That is
right for the reader who reads everything and wrong for the reader who scrolls on a
phone for ninety seconds. The page must serve both without becoming two pages.

### The skim layer (design this first)

- **One receipt line per section**, above the chart, not below it. A very large number
  and one active-voice sentence: *"+$83 a month. That is your household's share of his
  war and his tariffs since January 2025."* The current "What this shows" callouts hold
  the right sentences but sit under the chart where the skimmer never arrives. Move them
  up, make them the section's first element after the headline, and give them the
  visual weight of the number they explain.
- **A running total.** The household receipt (`receipt` block: fuel $36.15, groceries
  $31.64, electricity $15.39 = **$83.18 a month**, **$1,440 since the handover**, 17.3
  months) should be present from the masthead to the end — a thin sticky rail, a chip in
  the ticker, or a repeated "running total" strip between sections. Design decides the
  device. It is the one number a skimmer should leave with.
- **An end card.** After "the other side", before sources: *The bill* — eight numbers on
  one screen, shareable, the same figures as the OG card. This is the screenshot people
  post.
- **A section index.** Eleven sections is long. A compact progress/nav element listing
  the sections by their receipt number (not their title) lets the skimmer jump. Mobile
  first.
- Target: the skim path is eight numbers, readable in under a minute, no scrolling
  inside sections.

### The deep layer

- Everything currently in footnotes, method text and source lines stays — behind a
  **"Show the work"** disclosure per section (method, falsifier, series IDs, tiers,
  caveats, links). Design the disclosure so it reads as *available*, not hidden: a visible
  rule with the label, not a chevron.
- **Never collapsed:** the "honest part" panels and "the other side" section. They are
  part of the argument, not the appendix.
- The event study result ("5 of 7 pre-classified events moved the right way") and the
  method envelopes belong in the deep layer.

## 4. Section order

Proposed order, with the reason. The arc is: *what it costs you → where it came from →
whose decision → jobs → your money → the world's verdict → the proof → what went right →
check our work.* The control-group section moves down: it is the methodological spine
for the reader who wants proof, and it was costing the skimmer a screen of blue before
they reached anything that touched their life.

| # | Section | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **The bill Trump handed you** (masthead) | The running total and the crude chart. Lead with the cost to the reader, then the war. |
| — | Ticker | Unchanged device; copy from the receipt lines. |
| 02 | **Your receipt** (expanded shelf) | The most persuasive and least technical thing on the page. Everyone knows what coffee costs. |
| 03 | **The strait he shut is still shut** (simulation) | Moved up from 09. It is the mechanism that explains 02, it is the most engaging element, and it is where the war becomes visible. |
| 04 | **His war is in the tails. His tariffs are in the core.** | Attribution, right after the mechanism. The "not summed" honest panel stays. |
| 05 | **Nobody is hiring** | Jobs. |
| 06 | **What he did to your money** (the squeeze) | Rates, expectations, sentiment. Follows jobs naturally. |
| 07 | **The world routed around America** (trade) | First half of "the world's verdict". |
| 08 | **The gold is leaving New York** (gold) | Second half; the newest and most striking evidence, so it closes the consequences arc. |

## 5. Section briefs

### 02 · Your receipt — expand from four items to the full shelf

The current shelf shows four items on a shared $0–$10 bar scale. It is elegant and it is
too small a sample: it invites "you picked the four that went up". The BLS publishes
thirteen average prices; we hold all of them, plus diesel, jet fuel and airline fares.
Show them all, risers and fallers, in one artefact.

**Data (Jan 2025 → Jul 2026, BLS average price, actual dollars; diesel/jet EIA weekly):**

|---|---|---|---|
| Diesel, gal (EIA, to 31 Aug) | $3.72 | **$5.60** | **+51%** · record |
| Coffee, lb | $7.02 | $9.32 | +33% |
| Gasoline, gal | $3.21 | $4.09 | +27% |
| Airline fares (CPI, y/y, Jul) | — | — | +25.5% |
| Ground beef, lb | $5.55 | $6.89 | +24% |
| Round steak, lb | $11.97 | $14.59 | +22% |
| Electricity, kWh | $0.179 | $0.197 | +10% |
| Utility gas, therm | $1.55 | $1.70 | +10% |
| Milk, gal | $4.03 | $4.31 | +7% |
| Bananas, lb | $0.62 | $0.65 | +5% |
| Chicken breast, lb | $3.97 | $4.15 | +5% |
| Potatoes, lb | $2.05 | $2.00 | −2% |
| Bread, lb | $1.93 | $1.82 | −5% |
| Bacon, lb | $7.04 | $6.58 | −6% |
| Eggs, dozen | $4.95 | $2.19 | **−56%** · avian flu resolved, not policy |

**Design problem to solve:** a shared dollar scale no longer works (steak is $14.59,
electricity is 20 cents). Options we would accept, in order of preference:

1. **A literal till receipt.** Two price columns (Jan 2025 / now), a change chip per line,
   risers and fallers in the same type at the same size, fallers not greyed. A total line
   at the bottom that is the household receipt: **+$83.18 a month**. Ledger paper, mono
   figures, the crimson chip on the biggest risers. This is the device the whole page is
   named after and it currently appears nowhere.
2. A bar chart on **percent change**, not dollars, with the dollar pair as the label.
   Loses the "coffee costs more than beef" comparison the V4 spec valued; gains 15 items.
3. Two panels: the four biggest in dollars on the current bars, the rest as a receipt.

Whatever the form: the fallers must be present at equal weight, eggs must carry its
avian-flu note, and diesel should lead.

### 03 · The strait he shut is still shut — mostly copy and placement

The simulation is finished and runs on measured data (every daily close; IMF PortWatch
transit counts, pre-war 83 a day, now 4). Asks:

- Receipt line above it: *"83 ships a day before he ordered the strike. 4 a day now. He
  says 30 a night."*

- A **tanker-only** readout is available (`hormuz_transits.observations[].tanker`,

- Keep every existing label: "illustrative geometry", "not AIS data", "no verified queue
  count", "last published close".

### 07 · The world routed around America (trade) — refine

Built without a design pass; see `docs/screenshots/08-trade.png`. What works: WTO
columns, claim-vs-measured Hormuz bars, six tiles. Asks:

- **Hero it on the Hormuz bars.** The four-bar comparison (pre-war 83 · "30 a night" ·
  Kpler 13 · PortWatch 4.3) is the sharpest artefact on the page and is currently the
  right-hand panel. Make it the lead and give the hatched "claim" bar a legend entry that
  says *claim, not data*.
- **A flow, not tiles.** Six tiles read as a dashboard. Consider one horizontal flow:
  *strait (4 ships/day) → oil supply (−4.3 mb/d) → diesel (record) → shipping (Shanghai–
  Jebel Ali +6%, $8,254) → airfares (+25.5%) / airline profits (halved) → your receipt.*
  The tiles' numbers become nodes. Diesel is the hinge.
- Keep the WTO's own sentence as a pull quote: *"Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz
  collapsing from 138 commercial vessels per day to almost zero."*
- Data live in `context.trade.*`, `context.war.*`, `macro.series.trade_balance /
  imports / customs_duties`, `hormuz_transits`.

### 08 · The gold is leaving New York (gold) — refine

Built without a design pass; see `docs/screenshots/07-gold.png`. The argument in one
sentence: **the Fed books foreign gold at a price fixed in 1973, so its own table counts
ounces, and the ounces have left every month since August 2025.** That sentence is the
whole section and it currently takes a paragraph of caption to arrive.

- **Hero:** the ten monthly bars hanging down from a rule (Sep 2025 → Jun 2026: −35, −25,
  −29, −10, −15, 0, −5, −15, −10, −15 tonnes; total **−159 t**; 5,919 → 5,760). Make the
  "counted in ounces at a 1973 price — this cannot be a valuation effect" point a label on
  the axis, not a footnote. A vault or ledger metaphor is welcome; a gold-bar illustration
  is not (it reads as a coin advert).
- **Second chart, smaller:** Treasuries in Fed custody for foreign officials, monthly
  change in $bn (Jan +69, Feb −7, Mar −57, Apr +7, May −59, Jun −50; $2.78tn → $2.62tn).
  Mixed signs — show them honestly.
- **Four move cards** (Netherlands 86 t, France 129 t, India 55% → 22% abroad, Germany
  1,236 t *still there, no plan*). Germany is the tension card: design it as "not yet",
  not as a fifth departure.
- **The honest panel must be at full size and must not read as a retraction.** It says:
  gold is down 20% from its January record; the dollar index is up since the war began;
  the ECB calls the gold-over-Treasuries crossover "mainly valuation"; a Federal Reserve
  note of 3 Sep 2026 says the same. Then: none of that explains the ounce count above.
  Pattern to design: **"We checked the counter-argument"** — the Fed's point in their
  words, our reply in ours, same type size.
- Data live in `context.gold.*` (Fed Table 3.13 points, moves, ECB, FEDS note, price, TIC).

Do not restore the spring's +1.11 without saying why.

### 10 · What went right — unchanged in weight, retitled

Six cards live: eggs −56%/yr, core CPI 2.5%, median CPI 2.7%, August +162,000, S&P 500

*"Against us"*. Nothing else changes.

### New · The bill (end card)

One screen, eight numbers, same as the OG card, shareable at 1200×630 and as a phone
screenshot. Suggested eight: **+$83/month** · **$1,440 so far** · **$114.58 peak crude** ·
**$5.60 diesel (record)** · **42,474 jobs/month vs 320,938** · **27% long-term
unemployed** · **4 ships a day through Hormuz vs 83** · **−159 t of foreign gold out of
New York**. Design the card so any of the eight can change length without breaking.

## 6. Constraints (unchanged from V4, restated because they are load-bearing)

- Zero radius. Hard offset shadows, zero blur. Halftone grounds. Chivo 900 / Chivo Mono /
  Archivo, self-hosted. Container-query sizing in cqi, no media queries.
- **No fabricated numbers, ever.** Every figure is a live value from the snapshot or a
  cited entry in `context_figures.json` with source, URL and tier. Placeholder text in
  the design must be marked as such.
- **War and tariff effects are never summed.**
- **No queue count.** The vessel layer is illustrative and labelled.
- **The insider-trading probe never shares a visual frame with the paper-physical spread.**
- Missing data renders as a gap or an em-dash. October 2025 CPI does not exist.
- Tier labels stay visible where they exist (TIER 1 / TIER 2 on event cards).
- Mobile at 390px: zero horizontal overflow; every control ≥ 44px.

## 9. Acceptance

- A first-time reader on a phone can name the monthly cost, the crude peak, the ship
  count and the gold figure after sixty seconds of scrolling without opening anything.
- A hostile reader can find the six "against us" rows, the honest panels, the missing-
  October note and both corrections without opening anything.
- Every number on every artboard is either bound to a snapshot key or marked
  `PLACEHOLDER`.
- No headline without an actor.
